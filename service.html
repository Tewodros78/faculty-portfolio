Service and Mentoring Statement

I see service and mentoring as part of the same commitment as teaching and research: helping people and institutions use evidence well. My own path from a public university in Ethiopia to a PhD at Howard was possible because teachers and mentors gave their time to me, and I want to do the same for others.

As a lecturer at Salale University, I advised an average of six undergraduate and graduate students each year on their senior essays and theses. I helped students turn broad interests into answerable questions, choose appropriate data and methods, and write clearly. I also led community research on rural livelihood diversification with 400 households in four rural districts, which connected the university to the communities it serves. In my discipline, I serve as a referee for the Journal of Population Economics and as a discussant at professional conferences.
